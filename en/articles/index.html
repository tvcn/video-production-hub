You Cannot Write a ScriptIf you cannot write a script, board eight shots with one action each; action will force the lines. You Cannot Find ActorsNo actors? Shoot hands, backs, objects, and sound; a person can be complete without a face. You Only Have One RoomOne room works if it changes once during the film: messier, emptier, darker. You Have No LightsUse window, white wall, reflector, and phone light; find direction before intensity. Your Budget Is ZeroCut scale, not structure; a simple film you can finish beats a better one you cannot. You Are Shooting AloneUse a locked camera and continuous action; do not act, operate, and pull focus at once. You Only Have a PhoneLock exposure and white balance, then stabilize the phone; steady image and sound beat lenses. The Image Looks FlatFlat images usually lack directional light and separated layers, not a better camera. The Shadows Are DirtyLower ISO, add ambient light, and do not lift shadows hard; lifted black is noise. The Highlights Are GoneStop down, add ND, kill unnecessary practicals; clipped pixels do not come back. The Color Is OffFix white balance and mixed light before color wheels; grading a bad source gets dirtier. The Audio Has HissCheck room, air conditioning, cables, and gain; replace the mic last. Wind Noise OutsideUse a windshield and find shelter; post cannot recover words eaten by wind. The Actor Is StiffGive a concrete task and make them walk; movement loosens lines. The Lines Sound ReadCut explanation, add action, and give them a listener; more adjectives make it worse. A Scene Has No ChangeIf a scene changes nothing, cut it or let someone gain or lose something. The Opening Is Too SlowCut the first thirty seconds and start where someone is already acting; context can wait. The Ending Has No ForceWeak endings usually mean no cost; make the character choose, then stop. The Film Is Too LongFind repetition: if one idea appears twice, cut the weaker instance. You Have No CoverageCoverage fails when every shot is one size; add wide, close, and insert. You Crossed the AxisReturn to a wide or add a neutral shot; do not hard-cut reversed faces. Continuity BrokePhotograph the set and match props, wardrobe, hands, and table; continuity is records, not memory. The Transition Is JarringFind an action, sound, or shape match; white flashes and pushes work once. The Pacing Is FlatVary shot length; three same-length shots in a row lose attention. Music Covers the DialogueDrop music six dB, then ask whether the scene needs it at all. The Subtitles Are Hard to ReadCut words, raise contrast, avoid mouth and hands; thicker strokes do not fix it. The Thumbnail Gets No ClicksWeak thumbnails usually lack an action; show a face doing something. The Title Says NothingWrite who meets what result; more adjectives, fewer clicks. The Platform Rejected ItCheck images, text, music, and claims; fix one cause, not the whole film. The Client Keeps RevisingWrite the objective in one sentence and test every revision against it. There Is No TimeCut shots and locations, not sound or backup; post can be lean, media cannot. The Budget Is OverStop nonessential gear first; overruns usually come from scope, not unit price. The Crew Is FightingStop for ten minutes and write who is waiting on whom; do not assign blame in public. AI Looks UncannyCheck eyes, hands, lip sync, and motion; any one breaks the illusion. AI Characters DriftBuild a character sheet and carry one reference into every shot. AI Rights Are UnclearCheck training source, commercial terms, and likeness consent; if unclear, do not deliver.
