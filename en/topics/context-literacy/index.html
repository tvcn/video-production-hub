Media and ethics

Media, ethics, law and algorithms: read the image, and read the conditions, power and responsibility behind it.

20 guides · grouped by topic, not publish date

  1. Literacy / Media

    Media Literacy: Five Questions for Any Video

    Do not start with “is it real.” Start with: who made it, what was cut, what context, and why it reached me now.

  2. History / Rights

    Rights: Check Five Things Before Publishing

    Footage, music, fonts, likeness, and location are five separate permissions. CC is not a commercial license.

  3. History & Literacy / Media literacy

    Platform Rules: What Gets Throttled

    Platform limits are not magic: reposts, exaggerated promises, engagement bait, and sensitive images get throttled.

  4. History & Literacy / Law & ethics

    Accessible Captions: Let People Who Cannot Hear Follow

    Accessible captions tell Deaf viewers who speaks, what happens, and how it sounds, not just the words.

  5. History & Literacy / Media literacy

    Source Check: Who Posted It First?

    Find the first poster and the original file; a hundred thousand reposts prove nothing.

  6. History & Literacy / Media literacy

    Edit Manipulation: Which Sentence Was Cut?

    Editing manipulation often removes one sentence; cut the premise and the conclusion changes.

  7. History & Literacy / Media literacy

    Algorithm Feed: Why the Same Thing Again?

    Feeds amplify what holds attention; the longer you stay, the more it assumes you like it.

  8. History & Literacy / Media literacy

    Clickbait: What the Title Promises, What the Body Gives

    Clickbait promises a strong result and delivers a weak example; summarize title and body separately.

  9. History & Literacy / Media literacy

    Deepfake Detection: Look Beyond the Face

    Look beyond the face: teeth, ears, blinks, background text, and light direction.

  10. History & Literacy / Media literacy

    Statistics Video: Did the Chart Lie?

    Check where the axis starts, what the total is, and whether the same group is compared.

  11. History & Literacy / Media literacy

    Sponsorship Disclosure: Make the Ad Visible

    Disclose sponsorship before the recommendation, not in the last line of the description.

  12. History & Literacy / Media literacy

    News Video: Scene, Secondhand Source, Verification

    Separate scene, secondhand source, and verification; an unverified scene is just footage.

  13. Business / Legal

    Likeness, Location, and Minors: What a Release Must Say

    Consent to be filmed is not consent to be used commercially. Likeness, property, guardian consent for minors, and AI face/voice synthesis are four different clearances; a “consent to shoot” note will not save a paid ad.

  14. History & Literacy / Law & ethics

    Privacy and Blurring: When It Is Required

    Blurring is not politeness; addresses, plates, medical records, and minors need protection when identifiable.

  15. Business / Legal

    Contract Scope: Price the “One More Revision” Before It Happens

    The most common film contract dispute is not payment; it is scope. Unclear deliverables, revisions, rights, acceptance, and termination become unpaid overtime.

  16. Business / Legal

    Music Licensing: Sync, Performance, and Platform Libraries

    “I bought the song” does not mean “I can use it commercially.” Film projects must separate sync (recording into the film), performance (public playback), mechanical (copying audio), and master rights.

  17. History & Literacy / Law & ethics

    Location Release: What the Paper Says

    A location release states hours, areas, disruption, damage, and footage rights.

  18. Business / Legal

    AI Content Labels: Explicit, Implicit, and Platform Duties

    AI content is not compliant with a single “AI” tag. China mainland rules distinguish explicit and implicit labels, with duties for platforms, tool providers, and publishers; ads and news are stricter.

  19. History & Literacy / Law & ethics

    Documentary Ethics: Filming Vulnerable People

    Ask three things: does he know the consequence, can he withdraw, and can the film hurt him.

  20. History & Literacy / Law & ethics

    Data Security: Client Privacy and Leaks

    Data security covers drives, cloud, chat, and forwarding; client privacy is not only the final cut.
