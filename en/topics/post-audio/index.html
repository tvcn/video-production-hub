Post audio and subtitles

Noise reduction, mix, subtitles and delivery: fix what can be fixed, then add mood; subtitles are read, not transcribed.

20 guides · grouped by topic, not publish date

  1. Post / Editing

    The Rough Cut: Make the Film Work First

    A rough cut does one thing: let a stranger understand who wants what and what blocks them. Rhythm, grade, and score come later.

  2. Post / Sound

    Sound: Dialogue First, Music Later

    An audience can forgive a rough image, but not dialogue they cannot hear. Clean the voice first, then add music and atmosphere.

  3. Post / Color

    Color: Match First, Style Second

    Fix white balance, exposure, and shot matching before LUTs and looks. If shots do not match, more style only makes it messier.

  4. Post / Pacing

    Pacing Is Not Speed; It Is When They Want the Next Line

    Fast cuts do not make a film good. Cut the explanation, land cuts on movement, then decide where silence belongs.

  5. Post / Sound

    Fix the Noise Before You Build a Mood

    When a film sounds uncomfortable, the audience rarely says “the sound is bad.” They just stop watching. Fix noise, levels, and sync before reaching for music.

  6. Post / Compositing

    Compositing: Make Both Layers Believe the Same Light

    A clean key is not a composite. Match direction, softness, color temperature, and contact shadow before touching the edge.

  7. Post / Editing

    Let Sound Cross the Cut: J-cuts and L-cuts

    Picture does not have to cut at the same time as sound. Let the next scene arrive early, or the last one linger, and the cut stops hitting the audience.

  8. Post / Subtitles

    Subtitles: Help Them Read, Not Just Transcribe

    Subtitles are not a transcript. They have to land the meaning before the eye gives up: short lines, breaks at the right place, numbers and names that read at a glance.

  9. Post / Editing

    Cut Points: Action, Eye Line, Sound

    A scene that will not cut usually has the wrong cut point, not too little footage. At every cut, the eye, the ear, or an action needs somewhere to land.

  10. Post / Sound

    Mixing: Land the Dialogue Before the Music

    The audience can ignore the music; they cannot afford to miss the line. Level the dialogue first, then push the music down. Music is background, not a cover-up.

  11. Post / Sound

    Room Tone: Do Not Let the Picture Live in a Vacuum

    The room's sound is not noise; it is the space. In every location, record thirty seconds of nothing: no voices, no movement, just the room. When the dialogue stops, room tone takes over, and the cut stops sounding like a cut.

  12. Post / Editing

    Transitions: Mark Time, Do Not Hide It

    A dissolve, a fade, a wipe is not decoration. Every transition answers: how long, where, and who is still here. If it cannot answer, hard cut.

  13. Post / Titles

    Title Design: Readable Before Cool

    Title design must be readable before it is cool; if the name is missed, the style is wasted.

  14. Post / Editing

    Proxy Workflow: Cut First, Quality Later

    Proxies do not lower the final quality; they let the edit move. Keep the originals untouched.

  15. Post / Color

    Primary Correction: Exposure and White Balance

    Primary correction fixes exposure and white balance first. Do not hide technical problems under a look.

  16. Post / Sound

    Loudness: Platform Target and True Peak

    Deliver to the platform's loudness target and leave true-peak headroom. Loud is not good.

  17. Post / Editing

    Fine Cut: Remove the Rough Edges

    Fine cutting is not adding effects; it smooths every cut, breath, and extra half-second.

  18. Post / Editing

    Parallel Editing: When Do Two Lines Meet?

    Parallel editing is not back-and-forth; it makes the audience ask when the two lines will collide.

  19. Post / Editing

    Versioning: Never Name It Final

    Name versions by date, platform, duration, and change reason. Never "final final."

  20. Post / Color management

    Color Management: Put the LUT in the Right Place

    Color management is not applying a cinematic LUT. It answers what the source space is, what the monitor is, and what delivery needs, then puts the transform on the right node.
