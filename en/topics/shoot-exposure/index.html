Exposure and frame

Judgement：Exposure, composition, camera position and movement: make every take editable before chasing a style.

18 guides · ordered by problem, not publish date

    Shutter, aperture, and ISO are not three equal knobs. Set motion first, depth second, and use ISO last.

  2. Next: choose the moveComposition: Tell the Eye Where to Look

    Who should the eye hit first, and where does it go second? Composition is that order. Set the subject, then the layers.

    Camera Height: Lower Before You Change Lenses

    The same face at one inch lower reads as a different person. Height sets the power relationship before the shot size; a new lens cannot fix standing in the wrong place.

  6. Shoot / Lens

    Focal Length: Decide How Close the Audience Stands

    Wide is not “fit everything in,” and long is not “shoot far away.” Focal length decides how close the audience stands before it decides how the face and background stack. Walk the scene first, then change the lens.

  7. Shoot / Shot Size

    Shot Size: Decide How Much the Audience Sees

    Shot size is not a vocabulary list of wide, medium, and close. It sets the distance between the audience and the person: do they see where he stands, or only an eye? Ask what this cut needs before moving the camera back.

  8. Shooting / Camera

    Sensor Size: What the Frame Really Changes

    Sensor size changes angle, depth, and low light, not "cinematic feel" by itself.

  9. Shooting / Exposure

    Waveform: Exposure You Can Measure

    The waveform measures brightness, not beauty. Protect highlights first, then decide how much shadow to keep.

  10. Shooting / Exposure

    False Color: Find the Clip at a Glance

    False color turns brightness into bands, so clipped skin or a dead sky is obvious at a glance.

  11. Shooting / Exposure

    Log Footage: Shoot for the Grade

    Log is not "flat looks pro"; it preserves highlights and shadows for post, if exposure and color are handled.

  12. Shooting / Lighting

    Hard Light: The Shadow Is the Shape

    Hard light is not ugly; shadows shape the space. The problem is usually that the shadow lands in the wrong place.

  13. Shooting / Multicam

    Multicam Sync: Timecode and a Camera Map

    Draw the camera map, then sync timecode. The edit must know which camera stood where.

  14. Shooting / Multicam & live

    Multicam and Live: Lock Timecode, Return, and Recording First

    Multicam is not more cameras. It is three cameras on one timeline, one return, and one audio plan. If any link lacks a backup, directing turns into guessing.

  15. Shoot / Deep guide

    Camera move: handheld, stabiliser, coverage

    Camera movement is not motion for its own sake; it serves blocking, coverage and the cut.

  16. Shoot / Deep guide

    Multicam: sync, coverage, blocking

    Multicam is not more cameras; sync, shot-size division and repeatable blocking make it editable.

  17. Shoot / Deep guide

    Log and exposure: waveform, false colour, ND

    Log is not magic; exposure, ND and false colour must be decided together or post gets flat grey.

  18. Shooting / Exposure

    Low-Light Shooting: Noise, Shutter, Practicals

    Low light is not about raising ISO. Decide which blacks can stay black, then use practicals and shutter to buy back detail.
