Lighting

Judgement：A window, one lamp, one bounce: direction first, mood second; skin first, shape second.

10 guides · ordered by problem, not publish date

Read these five first

  1. Start: frame itLighting Ratio: Learn the Ratio Before the Positions

    One key and one reflector can shoot most portrait scenes. Control the contrast first, then collect positions.

  2. Next: choose the moveHow to Use One Window as Your Key Light

    You do not need a lighting kit for a face: set the direction, add a little bounce, then bring the background down.

  3. Then: failure modesLighting Style: Soft, Hard, Natural

    Pick one direction first: soft, hard, or natural. All three at once means no attitude.

  4. Check: prove itNight Interior: One Practical Can Carry a Room

    Find a practical light the room can believe, then add one small face light. Do not flatten the whole room.

  5. Cross-read: one moreMixed Light: Keep the Two Temperatures

    Do not white-balance mixed light into one color; keep the cool window and warm bulb for depth.

    Hard Light: The Shadow Is the Shape

    Hard light is not ugly; shadows shape the space. The problem is usually that the shadow lands in the wrong place.

    Practical Light: Use the Lamp in the Frame

    A lamp in frame must actually turn on, off, or flicker, but it motivates light rather than lighting the face.

  8. Shooting / LED virtual production

    LED Virtual Production: Wall, Tracking, Sync, and Color

    LED virtual production is not swapping a backdrop for a screen. It binds practical light, camera tracking, panel refresh, and color into one chain; if one link is loose, the light on the actor separates from the background.

  9. Shoot / Deep guide

    Hard light on location: hard, mixed, practical

    Hard light is not a defect; the question is whether hard, practical and colour are arranged on the same face.

  10. Shooting / Lighting

    Interview Lighting: Window, Single Lamp, Mixed Light

    Interview lighting is not about brightening a face. It makes the subject willing to talk and gives the edit reactions; set direction first, then ratio, then add lamps.
