Bodies and lenses

Judgement：Bodies, lenses, framing and support: ask what you shoot before what you buy; specs are not pictures.

    A follow focus gives marks and feel; autofocus is not reliable in darkness or fast motion.

    Check payload and head before max height; camera, lens, and accessories weigh more than the spec.

    Check controllability: can it lock character, camera, duration? Demo reels lie.

  9. Tools / Tutorial

    Camera Black Balance: Do Not Bake Noise

    Black balance as the camera requires; do not bake noise into the look. Clean shadows give post room.

  10. Tools / Tutorial

    Slider Shot: Head, Body, Tail

    A slider shot needs a head, body, and tail: hold the start, move evenly, hold the end.

  11. Tools / Buying guide

    Buying a Camera: Budget Before Body

    On a limited budget, the body is not first. Find whether you lack low light, autofocus, 10-bit, or audio. The wrong body forces every accessory to change with it.

  12. Tools / Buying & fixes

    Renting a Camera: Insurance, Deposit, Check

    Insurance, deposit, and a hands-on check; film scratches and dead pixels as evidence.

  13. Tools / Buying guide

    Buy the Lens First: Focal Length, Aperture, Breathing

    The lens decides half the image and holds value better than a body. Buy one focal length that covers 70% of your work, then a second; do not start with three cheap zooms.

  14. Tools / Buying guide

    Buy Audio First: Mic, Noise Floor, and Gain

    Audiences forgive a plain image; they do not forgive unclear sound. On a limited budget, put money into a microphone and recorder before a second lens.

  15. Tools / Buying guide

    Buying a Monitor: Brightness, Gamut, and Latency

    A monitor is not a bigger screen. It must be bright enough outdoors, calibratable, and low-latency for focus; if one fails, the camera screen is better.

  16. Tools / Buying & fixes

    Overheating Fix: Cool, Segment, Lower Bitrate

    Cool it, shoot in segments, lower bitrate; do not put a wet towel on the body.

  17. Tools / Buying & fixes

    Jelly Effect Fix: Read Speed and Shutter

    Rolling shutter comes from slow readout and fast motion; change body or shutter before blaming the lens.

  18. Tools / Buying guide

    Buying a Cinema Camera: “Can Shoot” Is Not “Can Deliver”

    A cinema camera’s price is not the body: cards, batteries, monitoring, rig, handles, cables, grading, and learning all count. Confirm the project needs RAW, timecode, SDI, and dual card, or a high-end video body is better value.

  19. Tools / Buying guide

    Buying Camera Cards: VPG, Sustained Write, and Heat

    A card’s peak read speed is irrelevant; sustained write decides whether frames drop. Match the camera’s minimum sustained write, buy one step above, and rotate at least two cards.

  20. Tools / Buying guide

    Your First Gimbal: Payload, Balance, and Follow

    A gimbal is not magic for walking. Payload headroom, three-axis balance, follow feel, battery, and axis locks decide whether you can finish a shot.
