TOPIC / tools

Support and stability

Tripods, gimbals, shoulder rigs and movement: stability is not an accessory, it is what makes a shot hold.

11 guides · grouped by topic, not publish date

  1. Tools / Capability card

    Tripod: Load and Head

    Check payload and head before max height; camera, lens, and accessories weigh more than the spec.

  2. Tools / Capability card

    Fluid Head: Pan and Tilt Need Drag

    A fluid head is about drag: starts and stops must be smooth, not snappy.

  3. Tools / Capability card

    Gimbal: Balance Before Power

    Balance the gimbal before powering up; motors fighting imbalance cause shake and battery drain.

  4. Tools / Tutorial

    Handheld Brace: Three Contact Points

    Find three contact points: chest, shoulder, elbow; three points stabilize the frame.

  5. Tools / Tutorial

    Gimbal Balance: Five Minutes Before Power

    Balance each axis with the others locked; five minutes before power saves half an hour on set.

  6. Tools / Tutorial

    Boom Swing: Follow the Chest

    Follow the chest, not the mouth; the mouth turns, the chest stays in range.

  7. Tools / Buying guide

    Buying a Tripod: Payload, Torsion, and Height

    A tripod’s payload rating is not a safety line. Leg stiffness, head lock, torsional rigidity, and centre of gravity decide stability; a flexy tripod rated 20kg still shakes in wind.

  8. Tools / Buying guide

    Buying LED Lights: Output, Flicker, and Power

    LED specs are not just wattage. Flicker, color shift, battery runtime, and DMX/wireless control decide whether it works on a real set.

  9. Tools / Buying guide

    Buying a Fluid Head: Drag, Counterbalance, and Return

    A fluid head is not “it pans.” The difference is continuous tilt drag, enough counterbalance, and a smooth return when released; these decide whether your pan/tilt is usable.

  10. Tools / Buying guide

    Your First Gimbal: Payload, Balance, and Follow

    A gimbal is not magic for walking. Payload headroom, three-axis balance, follow feel, battery, and axis locks decide whether you can finish a shot.

  11. Tools / Buying guide

    Buying Timecode: Drift, Sync, and Battery

    Timecode is not automatic for multicam, but when needed, drift and sync stability matter more than looks. Test eight-hour drift before deciding how many units.
